The Seventh Planet

Uranus

Meet Uranus, a distant ice giant with a pale blue-green atmosphere, a faint ring system and an extraordinary sideways rotation. Its unusual seasons make it one of the most mysterious planets in our Solar System.

A Unique Ice Giant

Meet Uranus

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun. It is classified as an ice giant because its interior contains large amounts of hot, dense materials such as water, ammonia and methane. Uranus appears blue-green because methane in its atmosphere absorbs red light.

Inside the Ice Giant

The Structure of Uranus

Uranus does not have a solid surface that a spacecraft could land on. Its atmosphere gradually transitions into deeper, hotter layers under enormous pressure.

01. Atmosphere

The outer atmosphere is made mainly of hydrogen and helium, with methane contributing to its blue-green colour.

02. Icy Mantle

Beneath the atmosphere lies a hot, dense interior containing water, ammonia and methane-rich materials.

03. Rocky Core

Scientists expect a dense central region containing heavier materials, but the planet's exact internal structure remains uncertain.

A Planet on Its Side

Uranus's Unusual Rotation

Uranus has an axial tilt of about 98 degrees. Instead of spinning more upright like most planets, it rotates almost on its side. Scientists think a major collision early in its history may have contributed to this unusual orientation.

98°

Axial Tilt

Uranus's extreme tilt creates unusual seasonal patterns as it travels around the Sun.

How does this affect Uranus?

  • Extreme seasons: Each season lasts approximately 21 Earth years.
  • Polar sunlight: During parts of its orbit, one pole can face the Sun for many years.
  • Retrograde rotation: Uranus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets when described using its tilted axis convention.
  • Mysterious history: Its unusual tilt provides clues about the early Solar System.
Weather and Climate

The Atmosphere of Uranus

Uranus may look calm compared with some other giant planets, but its atmosphere contains clouds, winds and changing weather. It is also one of the coldest planetary environments in the Solar System.

Blue-Green Appearance

Methane absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight, helping Uranus appear blue-green to observers.

Very Cold Clouds

Uranus's upper atmosphere reaches extremely low temperatures, making it one of the coldest planetary atmospheres measured.

Changing Weather

Telescopes have observed bright clouds and atmospheric features that change over time.

A Hidden Ring System

Uranus's Rings

Uranus has a system of faint, narrow rings. Unlike Saturn's bright rings, Uranus's rings are dark and difficult to observe without powerful telescopes or spacecraft.

Dark Particles

The rings contain dark material that reflects relatively little sunlight.

Narrow Rings

Many of Uranus's rings are narrow and sharply defined compared with Saturn's broad ring system.

Discovered in 1977

Uranus's rings were discovered when astronomers observed a star briefly dimming as Uranus passed in front of it.

Natural Satellites

Uranus's Moons

Uranus has a diverse collection of moons. Many are named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope.

Titania

Titania is the largest moon of Uranus. Its surface contains large valleys, faults and impact craters.

Oberon

Oberon is Uranus's second-largest moon and has an ancient, heavily cratered surface.

Miranda

Miranda is known for its unusual surface, including large cliffs, deep valleys and complex terrain.

Ariel

Ariel has a relatively bright icy surface marked by valleys, canyons and impact features.

Umbriel

Umbriel has a dark surface with many impact craters, making it one of Uranus's darker large moons.

More Moons

Uranus has many smaller moons in addition to its five major moons. Observations continue to reveal more about this distant system.

Journey Beyond Earth

Exploring Uranus

Uranus has been visited closely by only one spacecraft. Modern telescopes continue to observe the planet and help scientists understand its atmosphere, rings and moons.

Voyager 2 — 1986

NASA's Voyager 2 made the first and so far only close flyby of Uranus. It studied the planet's atmosphere, rings, magnetic field and moons.

Hubble Space Telescope

Hubble has helped astronomers observe Uranus's atmospheric changes, clouds, rings and auroras.

Future Exploration

Scientists have proposed future missions to study Uranus in greater detail, especially its interior, atmosphere and complex moon system.

Quick Discoveries

Interesting Facts About Uranus

1Sideways Planet

Uranus rotates almost on its side because its axial tilt is about 98 degrees.

2Long Seasons

Each season lasts about 21 Earth years because Uranus takes roughly 84 years to orbit the Sun.

3Discovered by Telescope

William Herschel discovered Uranus in 1781, making it the first planet discovered with a telescope.

4Ice Giant

Uranus and Neptune are ice giants, while Jupiter and Saturn are classified as gas giants.

5Faint Rings

Uranus has a dark, faint ring system that is much harder to see than Saturn's rings.

6Low Temperatures

Uranus's atmosphere can reach temperatures of around 49 kelvin, or about -224°C, in its coldest measured regions.
