The Jewel of the Solar System

Saturn

Discover Saturn, the magnificent gas giant famous for its spectacular rings. With its golden atmosphere, fascinating moons, and mysterious hexagonal storm, Saturn is one of the most remarkable worlds in our solar system.

Explore Saturn ↓
01 / Overview

The Ringed Giant

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our solar system.

What is Saturn?

Saturn is a gas giant composed mostly of hydrogen and helium. It does not have a solid surface like Earth. Its atmosphere contains clouds, strong winds, and storms.

Saturn is best known for its extensive ring system, which contains countless pieces of ice, rocky material, and dust orbiting the planet.

Why is Saturn special?

  • It has the most visually prominent ring system among the planets.
  • It is the second-largest planet in the solar system.
  • It has a large and diverse collection of moons.
  • Its north pole has a famous hexagon-shaped atmospheric pattern.
  • Its moon Titan has lakes and seas of liquid hydrocarbons.

02 / Structure

Inside Saturn

Beneath Saturn's beautiful clouds lies an enormous interior of hydrogen, helium, and heavier materials under extreme pressure.

01

Upper Atmosphere

Saturn's outer atmosphere contains clouds dominated by ammonia ice, with other cloud layers deeper below.

02

Molecular Hydrogen

Most of Saturn's outer interior contains hydrogen and helium. The gases become denser as pressure increases toward the center.

03

Deep Interior

At greater depths, hydrogen experiences extreme pressure and may enter electrically conducting states.

04

Heavy-Element Core Region

Saturn is believed to contain heavier elements deep inside. Scientists are still investigating the structure and mixing of its central region.

Did you know? Saturn has a very low average density compared with water. However, it is a giant planet, and it could not simply float in an ordinary ocean.

03 / The Rings

Saturn's Spectacular Rings

Saturn's rings stretch across a vast region around the planet and contain particles of many different sizes.

What are the rings made of?

Saturn's rings are made mostly of water ice, along with smaller amounts of rocky material and dust. Individual particles range from tiny grains to much larger chunks.

Although the rings look solid in photographs, they consist of countless separate particles orbiting Saturn.

Ring Divisions

Astronomers identify several main rings, including the A, B, and C rings. The Cassini Division is a prominent gap between the A and B rings, although it is not completely empty.

The rings are extremely wide but, in many places, remarkably thin compared with their overall span.

Saturn's rings are not permanent structures on astronomical timescales. Research suggests that ring material gradually falls toward the planet, and the rings may change substantially over time.

04 / Moons

Worlds Around Saturn

Saturn has a remarkable collection of moons, from worlds with thick atmospheres to icy bodies with hidden oceans.

🌫️

Titan

Titan is Saturn's largest moon. It has a thick atmosphere and lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane on its surface.

💧

Enceladus

Enceladus ejects plumes of water-rich material into space. Evidence indicates that a global ocean exists beneath its icy crust.

🌑

Rhea

Rhea is one of Saturn's larger moons. Its icy surface is marked by numerous impact craters.

🧊

Dione

Dione is an icy moon with a heavily cratered surface and bright fractures in some regions.

🌌

Iapetus

Iapetus is known for its striking contrast between a dark hemisphere and a much brighter one.

🪨

Mimas

Mimas has a huge impact crater that gives it a distinctive appearance in spacecraft images.

05 / Atmosphere

Storms and Strange Weather

Saturn's atmosphere contains powerful winds, changing cloud patterns, and unusual polar features.

The North Pole Hexagon

A remarkable six-sided jet stream surrounds Saturn's north polar region. This enormous hexagonal pattern has been observed by spacecraft and telescopes.

Powerful Winds

Saturn's atmosphere has very strong winds and broad bands of clouds. Large storms can develop and change over time, revealing the planet's active weather system.

06 / Exploration

Discovering Saturn

Space missions have transformed our understanding of Saturn, its rings, and its moons.

Pioneer 11

NASA's Pioneer 11 spacecraft flew past Saturn in 1979, providing important early observations of the planet and its surroundings.

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

The Voyager spacecraft conducted close flybys of Saturn, revealing more details about its atmosphere, rings, and moons.

Cassini

The Cassini spacecraft orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017. It studied the planet, rings, and moons in great detail.

Huygens

The Huygens probe descended through Titan's atmosphere and landed on its surface in 2005, returning valuable information about this unusual moon.

Cassini's final journey: In 2017, Cassini entered Saturn's atmosphere at the end of its mission, helping protect the planet's moons from possible contamination.

07 / Discoveries

Interesting Facts

Saturn has many extraordinary features beyond its famous rings.

💍

Famous Rings

Saturn's bright rings make it one of the most recognizable planets in the solar system.

⏳

A Long Year

Saturn takes about 29.4 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun.

🌪️

Polar Hexagon

Saturn's north pole contains a remarkable six-sided atmospheric jet stream.

🌊

Hidden Oceans

Enceladus has a subsurface ocean and ejects water-rich material through cracks in its ice.

🌫️

Orange Titan

Titan's thick atmosphere gives the moon a hazy orange appearance.

🔭

Visible from Earth

Saturn can be seen without a telescope under suitable conditions. A telescope can reveal its beautiful rings.
