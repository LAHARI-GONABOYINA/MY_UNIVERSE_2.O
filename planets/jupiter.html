The Giant of Our Solar System

Jupiter

Meet Jupiter, the largest planet in our solar system. This magnificent gas giant is famous for its colorful cloud bands, enormous storms, powerful magnetic field, and fascinating family of moons.

Explore Jupiter ↓
01 / Overview

A World of Giants

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the biggest planet in our planetary neighborhood.

What is Jupiter?

Jupiter is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Unlike Earth, it does not have a solid surface that we could stand on. Its atmosphere contains colorful clouds, powerful winds, and giant storms.

Jupiter's immense gravity influences many objects in the solar system, making it an important planet for scientists to study.

Why is Jupiter special?

  • It is the largest planet in the solar system.
  • It has the famous Great Red Spot storm.
  • It has a large family of moons.
  • It possesses a powerful magnetic field.
  • It has a faint ring system.

02 / Structure

Inside Jupiter

Jupiter's interior contains layers of gas and exotic forms of matter under extreme pressure and temperature.

01

Outer Atmosphere

The visible cloud tops contain ammonia ice, ammonium hydrosulfide, and water-related clouds. Different cloud layers help create Jupiter's colorful appearance.

02

Molecular Hydrogen

Deeper inside, hydrogen gas becomes increasingly dense as pressure and temperature rise.

03

Metallic Hydrogen

Under enormous pressure, hydrogen behaves like an electrically conducting fluid. Its movement helps generate Jupiter's powerful magnetic field.

04

Central Heavy-Element Region

Jupiter may have a diffuse, partially mixed central region containing heavier elements. Scientists are still investigating its exact internal structure.

Did you know? Jupiter releases more energy than it receives from the Sun, partly because it slowly releases heat left over from its formation.

03 / Atmosphere

Clouds, Winds and Storms

Jupiter's atmosphere is a constantly changing world of moving clouds, strong winds, and enormous storms.

Colorful Cloud Bands

Jupiter has alternating light and dark bands that circle the planet. These bands are shaped by atmospheric circulation and powerful jet streams. Their colors are influenced by cloud chemistry and other atmospheric processes.

The Great Red Spot

The Great Red Spot is a gigantic storm in Jupiter's atmosphere. It has been observed for centuries and is large enough to contain Earth, although its size changes over time.

Jupiter's atmosphere has no ordinary solid ground. A spacecraft descending into it would encounter increasing pressure and temperature.

04 / Moons

A Family of Moons

Jupiter has many natural satellites, including four famous large moons discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610.

🌋

Io

Io is the most volcanically active world known. Jupiter's gravity helps drive its intense volcanic activity.

🌊

Europa

Europa has an icy outer shell and strong evidence for a global ocean beneath its surface, making it an important target in the search for habitable environments.

🌑

Ganymede

Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system. It is even larger in diameter than the planet Mercury.

❄️

Callisto

Callisto is heavily covered with impact craters and preserves a very old surface.

💍

Jupiter's Rings

Jupiter has a faint ring system made mostly of dust. Its rings are much less visible than Saturn's.

🧲

Magnetic Field

Jupiter's powerful magnetic field creates an enormous magnetosphere around the planet.

05 / Orbit and Rotation

Jupiter's Journey

Jupiter spins rapidly but takes almost twelve Earth years to complete one trip around the Sun.

A Fast-Spinning Giant

Jupiter completes one rotation in roughly ten hours, making it the fastest-spinning planet in our solar system. Its rapid rotation contributes to its noticeably flattened shape at the poles.

A Long Year

Jupiter takes about 11.86 Earth years to orbit the Sun. Because it is much farther from the Sun than Earth, its orbital journey is much longer.

06 / Exploration

Exploring Jupiter

Space missions have helped scientists understand Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, rings, and moons.

Pioneer 10 and Pioneer 11

These early NASA missions flew past Jupiter and provided important information about the planet and its environment.

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

The Voyager spacecraft observed Jupiter and its moons in greater detail, revealing complex atmospheric activity and active volcanic features on Io.

Galileo

NASA's Galileo mission studied Jupiter and its moons for years. It also sent an atmospheric probe into Jupiter's atmosphere.

Juno

NASA's Juno spacecraft has studied Jupiter's gravity, magnetic field, atmosphere, and interior structure from orbit.

Europa Clipper

NASA's Europa Clipper mission is traveling to Jupiter to investigate Europa's ice shell, subsurface ocean, and potential habitability.

JUICE

The European Space Agency's Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer mission is on its journey to study Jupiter and its large icy moons.

07 / Discoveries

Interesting Facts

Jupiter is full of surprising features that make it one of the most fascinating planets to explore.

🌌

The Largest Planet

Jupiter is so large that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it by volume, approximately.

💨

Powerful Winds

Jupiter's atmosphere contains fast-moving winds and long-lasting storms.

✨

Bright in Our Sky

Jupiter can appear very bright in Earth's night sky and can often be seen without a telescope.

🪐

Faint Rings

Jupiter's rings are difficult to see from Earth because they are faint and mostly made of dust.

🧊

Potentially Habitable Moons

Europa and Ganymede are important scientific targets because evidence suggests they contain oceans beneath their icy surfaces.

🔭

Still Full of Mysteries

Scientists continue to investigate Jupiter's deep interior, atmosphere, magnetic field, and complex moon system.
