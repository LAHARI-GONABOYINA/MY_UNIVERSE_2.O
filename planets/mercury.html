A Small but Fascinating World

Meet Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System. It has a rocky surface covered with craters, a large metallic core and almost no substantial atmosphere. Because it is so close to the Sun, its surface experiences extreme temperature changes between day and night.

Inside the Planet

The Structure of Mercury

Mercury is a terrestrial planet with a rocky outer layer and a disproportionately large iron-rich core. Its internal structure gives scientists important clues about how rocky planets formed.

01. Crust

Mercury's outermost rocky layer contains impact craters, plains and long cliffs formed as the planet cooled and contracted.

02. Mantle

Beneath the crust lies a rocky mantle that surrounds Mercury's large metallic core.

03. Iron Core

Mercury has a large iron-rich core that occupies a substantial fraction of its interior and helps generate a global magnetic field.

A World of Craters

Mercury's Rocky Surface

Mercury's surface preserves a long history of impacts from asteroids and comets. Since the planet has almost no atmosphere to erode its surface, many ancient craters remain visible.

Impact Craters

Numerous craters cover Mercury, showing the effects of collisions throughout its history.

Caloris Basin

The Caloris Basin is a huge impact feature formed by an ancient collision with a large object.

Long Cliffs

Large cliffs, called lobate scarps, formed as Mercury gradually cooled and shrank.

Extreme Conditions

Hot Days and Cold Nights

Mercury has almost no substantial atmosphere to distribute heat around the planet. As a result, its sunlit surface can become extremely hot, while its nighttime surface becomes very cold.

430°C

Approximate Daytime High

Temperatures on the sunlit surface can rise to around 430°C.

Why are temperatures so extreme?

  • Thin exosphere: Mercury does not have a thick atmosphere to retain and spread heat.
  • Slow rotation: Its long solar day means a location experiences extended periods of sunlight and darkness.
  • Cold polar craters: Some deep craters near the poles never receive direct sunlight.
  • Possible water ice: Scientists have found evidence of water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters.
Around the Sun

Mercury's Orbit and Rotation

Mercury travels around the Sun faster than any other planet. Its orbit is elliptical, and its rotation and revolution combine to create a solar day that lasts longer than its year.

Fastest Orbit

Mercury completes one trip around the Sun in about 88 Earth days.

Long Solar Day

One sunrise-to-sunrise cycle on Mercury lasts about 176 Earth days.

3:2 Spin-Orbit Resonance

Mercury rotates three times for every two orbits around the Sun.

A Hidden Force

Mercury's Magnetic Field

Although Mercury is small, it has a global magnetic field. It is much weaker than Earth's magnetic field, but it provides clues about the planet's deep interior.

Global Field

Mercury has a magnetic field that interacts with the solar wind flowing outward from the Sun.

Large Metallic Core

The planet's iron-rich interior is important for understanding how its magnetic field is produced.

Solar Wind

Mercury's magnetic environment is affected by charged particles emitted by the Sun.

Exploring Mercury

Space Missions

Spacecraft have helped scientists map Mercury's surface, measure its magnetic field and investigate its internal structure.

Mariner 10

NASA's Mariner 10 flew past Mercury three times during 1974 and 1975, returning the first close-up images of the planet.

MESSENGER

NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015 and mapped much of its surface.

BepiColombo

The European-Japanese BepiColombo mission is designed to study Mercury's surface, interior, magnetic field and surrounding environment.

Quick Discoveries

Interesting Facts About Mercury

1Smallest Planet

Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System.

2Fastest Planet

Mercury has the highest average orbital speed of all the planets.

3No Natural Moons

Mercury has no confirmed natural satellites.

4Ice Near the Sun

Water ice can persist in permanently shadowed craters near Mercury's poles.

5Ancient Surface

Its many impact craters preserve evidence of ancient collisions in the Solar System.

6Not the Hottest

Venus is hotter on average because its thick atmosphere traps heat much more effectively.
