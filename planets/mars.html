PLANET MARS

Welcome to Mars, the fourth planet from the Sun. Known as the Red Planet, Mars has a dusty landscape, enormous volcanoes, deep canyons, polar ice caps, and evidence that liquid water once flowed across parts of its surface. Discover a world that may hold clues to the history of life beyond Earth.

01 / PLANET PROFILE

Meet Mars

Mars is a cold, rocky world with a thin atmosphere and surface features that preserve evidence of a very different past.

The Red Planet

Mars gets its reddish appearance from iron-rich minerals in its dust and rocks. It has mountains, valleys, impact craters, and ancient river-like channels that scientists study to understand its geological history.


Today, Mars is cold and dry at the surface. Water exists mainly as ice, and its thin atmosphere cannot retain heat as effectively as Earth's atmosphere.

02 / INSIDE THE PLANET

The Internal Structure of Mars

Like Earth, Mars has a crust, mantle, and core. Scientists use spacecraft measurements to investigate its interior.

01

Core

Mars has a metallic core containing iron and other elements. NASA's InSight mission helped scientists investigate the planet's interior structure.

02

Mantle

The mantle lies beneath the crust and consists of rocky material. Its thermal and geological history helps scientists understand how Mars evolved.

03

Crust

Mars's crust contains ancient rocks, volcanic plains, impact craters, and geological formations shaped over billions of years.

04

Surface Dust

Fine, iron-rich dust covers large areas of Mars. Winds move this dust around and can produce dust storms that sometimes spread across the planet.

Scientific discovery: NASA's InSight lander used seismic measurements to study marsquakes and learn more about the planet's internal structure.
03 / WHAT IS MARS MADE OF?

Composition of Mars

Mars contains rocky material, iron-rich dust, polar ice, and a thin atmosphere.

🪨

Rocky Surface

Mars has rocky terrain, mountains, plains, impact craters, and large volcanic structures.

🔴

Iron-Rich Dust

Oxidized iron minerals in Martian dust contribute to the planet's familiar red-orange color.

❄️

Water Ice

Water ice exists in polar deposits and beneath parts of the surface. Scientists also study evidence of ancient liquid water.

04 / A COLD DESERT WORLD

Atmosphere and Surface

Mars has a thin atmosphere and a landscape shaped by impacts, wind, and ancient water.

🌬️

Thin Atmosphere

Mars's atmosphere consists mainly of carbon dioxide. Its low pressure provides little protection against the harsh surface environment.

🌋

Olympus Mons

Olympus Mons is a gigantic shield volcano and one of the largest known volcanoes in the solar system.

🏜️

Valles Marineris

Valles Marineris is an immense canyon system stretching across a large part of the Martian surface.

Did you know? Mars has seasons because its axis is tilted, much like Earth's. However, its year is longer, so its seasons last longer too.
05 / COSMIC MOVEMENT

Rotation and Orbit

Mars has a day length similar to Earth's, but its journey around the Sun takes much longer.

A Martian Day

Mars rotates once in approximately 24 hours and 37 minutes. Scientists often call a Martian solar day a "sol."

A Martian Year

Mars takes approximately 687 Earth days to complete one orbit around the Sun. This makes its year nearly twice as long as an Earth year.

Seasons on Mars

Mars has spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Its axial tilt produces seasonal changes in temperature, polar ice, and atmospheric activity.

Dust Storms

Winds can lift dust into the atmosphere. Some storms grow large enough to affect much of the planet and reduce sunlight reaching the surface.

06 / MARTIAN MOONS

Phobos and Deimos

Mars has two small natural moons that orbit close to the planet.

Phobos

Phobos is the larger and closer of Mars's two moons. It orbits Mars very quickly and is gradually moving closer to the planet over time.

Deimos

Deimos is the smaller and more distant moon. It has an irregular shape and moves around Mars more slowly than Phobos does.

Moon mystery: Scientists continue to investigate how Phobos and Deimos formed and whether they are captured asteroids or the products of another process.
07 / WHY MARS MATTERS

Scientific Importance of Mars

Mars helps scientists investigate planetary evolution, ancient environments, and the possibility of life beyond Earth.

💧

Searching for Ancient Water

River channels, minerals, and sedimentary rocks provide evidence that liquid water existed on ancient Mars.

🧬

Searching for Signs of Life

Robotic missions investigate whether ancient Martian environments could have supported microbial life. Life has not yet been confirmed on Mars.

🚀

Future Human Exploration

Mars research helps scientists and engineers understand the challenges of future human missions to another planet.

08 / EXPLORING MARS

Important Space Missions

Orbiters, landers, and rovers have transformed our understanding of the Red Planet.

Mariner 4

NASA's Mariner 4 performed the first successful close flyby of Mars in 1965 and returned the first close-up images of another planet's surface.

Viking Missions

NASA's Viking 1 and Viking 2 missions included orbiters and landers that studied the surface, atmosphere, and possible signs of life.

Curiosity Rover

Curiosity studies Martian geology and past environmental conditions to investigate whether Mars once had conditions suitable for microbial life.

Perseverance Rover

Perseverance explores Jezero Crater, studies ancient rocks, and collects samples for possible future return to Earth.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

This NASA orbiter uses powerful instruments to study the surface, atmosphere, and potential landing locations on Mars.

Mangalyaan

India's Mars Orbiter Mission, also called Mangalyaan, successfully entered Mars orbit in 2014 and demonstrated India's interplanetary mission capability.
