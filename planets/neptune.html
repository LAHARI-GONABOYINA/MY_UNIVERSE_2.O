The Eighth Planet

Neptune

Far beyond Uranus lies Neptune, a deep-blue world of powerful winds, icy clouds, dark storms and mysterious moons. It is the farthest recognized planet from the Sun.

A Distant World

Meet Neptune

Neptune is an ice giant in the outer Solar System. Although it looks calm from a distance, its atmosphere contains some of the fastest winds measured on any planet. Sunlight is extremely weak there, and one orbit around the Sun takes about 165 Earth years.

Beneath the Clouds

Inside Neptune

Neptune does not have a solid surface like Earth. Its visible clouds cover deep layers of gas and hot, dense material.

01. Upper Atmosphere

The outer atmosphere contains hydrogen, helium and methane. Methane absorbs red light and helps give Neptune its blue appearance.

02. Deep Mantle

Beneath the atmosphere is a hot, dense region containing water, ammonia and methane-rich materials under extreme pressure.

03. Inner Core

Neptune is thought to have a dense rocky core surrounded by hotter layers. Its exact internal structure is still being studied.

A Dynamic Atmosphere

Clouds, Winds and Storms

Neptune is one of the windiest worlds in our Solar System. Its atmosphere features bright clouds, dark storm systems and changing weather patterns.

2,000+

km/h Winds

Some winds on Neptune can exceed 2,000 kilometres per hour. The exact speed varies with location and measurement.

What makes Neptune special?

  • Blue colour: Methane absorbs much of the red light in sunlight, while atmospheric processes also contribute to Neptune's colour.
  • Dark storms: Large storm systems can form and change over time in its atmosphere.
  • Bright clouds: High-altitude clouds of frozen methane can appear against the blue background.
  • Internal heat: Neptune radiates more energy than it receives from the Sun.
Natural Satellites

Neptune's Moons

Neptune has 14 known moons. Triton is the largest and most unusual because it travels around Neptune in the opposite direction to the planet's rotation.

Triton

Neptune's largest moon has a very cold surface and nitrogen ice. Voyager 2 observed geyser-like eruptions on Triton in 1989.

Nereid

Nereid follows an unusually elongated orbit, taking it much closer to and farther from Neptune during its journey.

Proteus

Proteus is one of Neptune's largest known moons and has a heavily cratered, irregular surface.

Larissa

Larissa is an irregular inner moon that orbits relatively close to Neptune.

Hippocamp

Hippocamp is a small moon discovered in images from the Hubble Space Telescope.

Other Moons

The remaining moons have different sizes, shapes and orbits. Continued observations help scientists understand their origins.

Around the Planet

Neptune's Faint Rings

Neptune has a faint ring system made of dust and darker material. The rings are difficult to see from Earth and were studied in detail by the Voyager 2 spacecraft.

Adams Ring

The Adams ring is one of Neptune's most prominent rings. It contains several brighter arcs.

Dusty Material

Neptune's rings contain dust and small particles that reflect little sunlight, making the rings faint.

Ring Arcs

Some sections of the Adams ring appear brighter than others. Their structure is influenced by Neptune's moons and gravity.

Exploring the Unknown

Space Missions

Neptune is so distant that only one spacecraft has flown past it. Telescopes continue to help scientists study the planet from afar.

Voyager 2 — 1989

Voyager 2 made the first and, so far, only close flyby of Neptune. It studied the atmosphere, rings and moons.

Hubble Space Telescope

Hubble has monitored Neptune's changing clouds, storms and atmospheric features over many years.

Earth-Based Telescopes

Powerful observatories use visible and infrared light to study Neptune's weather and atmospheric changes.

Quick Discoveries

Interesting Facts About Neptune

1Very Far Away

Neptune is about 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth is on average.

2Long Year

Since Neptune takes about 165 Earth years to orbit the Sun, its seasons last for decades.

3First Predicted

Mathematical calculations helped scientists predict Neptune's position before it was observed in 1846.

4Ice Giant

Neptune is classified as an ice giant, unlike Jupiter and Saturn, which are classified as gas giants.

5Captured Triton?

Triton's unusual backward orbit suggests it may have been captured by Neptune's gravity long ago.

6Powerful Weather

Despite receiving very little sunlight, Neptune has an active atmosphere with fast winds and changing storms.
