SOLAR SYSTEM / PLANET 02

THE VENUS

Welcome to Venus, the second planet from the Sun. Known as Earth's sister planet because of its similar size, Venus is a world of crushing atmospheric pressure, thick clouds, and extreme temperatures. Explore the mysteries of our solar system's hottest planet.

01 / PLANET PROFILE

Meet Venus

A rocky planet hidden beneath a thick atmosphere, Venus is one of the most fascinating worlds in our celestial neighborhood.

A World of Extreme Heat

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is similar to Earth in size and internal structure. However, its atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, with thick clouds of sulfuric acid. This atmosphere traps heat through an intense greenhouse effect, making Venus the hottest planet in the solar system.


The planet has a rocky surface, mountains, plains, and evidence of extensive volcanic activity. Its harsh environment makes surface exploration extremely challenging.

02 / INSIDE THE PLANET

The Internal Structure of Venus

Venus is a terrestrial planet with a layered interior believed to be broadly similar to Earth's.

01

Inner Core

Venus is believed to have a metallic core composed mainly of iron and nickel. Its exact state and structure remain uncertain.

02

Outer Core

The state and behavior of Venus's core are not fully understood. Unlike Earth, Venus does not have a strong global magnetic field.

03

Mantle

Beneath the crust lies a thick rocky mantle. Heat inside the planet may drive geological processes and volcanic activity.

04

Crust

The outer rocky layer contains plains, mountains, volcanic features, and impact craters shaped over geological time.

Scientific note: Scientists continue to investigate Venus's interior. Some details about its core and geological activity are still uncertain.
03 / WHAT IS VENUS MADE OF?

Composition of Venus

Venus consists of a rocky interior and a dense atmosphere that creates an extreme environment.

🪨

Rocky Surface

Venus has a solid surface with mountains, volcanic plains, valleys, and impact craters.

🌫️

Carbon Dioxide

The atmosphere consists mainly of carbon dioxide, which contributes to the planet's powerful greenhouse effect.

☁️

Sulfuric Acid Clouds

Thick clouds containing sulfuric acid droplets surround Venus and obscure its surface from ordinary visible-light observations.

04 / A HOSTILE WORLD

Atmosphere and Surface

Venus has one of the most extreme planetary environments in the solar system.

🔥

Extreme Temperatures

The average surface temperature is around 465°C. Venus is hotter than Mercury even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

🌪️

Crushing Pressure

Atmospheric pressure at the surface is about 92 times Earth's sea-level pressure, creating a very difficult environment for landers.

🌋

Volcanic Landscape

Venus has extensive volcanic features. Researchers study its surface to understand its geological history and possible ongoing volcanic activity.

Why is Venus so hot? Its thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere traps heat, producing an extreme greenhouse effect that keeps the surface hotter than Mercury's.
05 / COSMIC MOVEMENT

Rotation and Orbit

Venus behaves differently from most planets when it comes to rotation and the length of a day.

Orbit Around the Sun

Venus completes one orbit around the Sun in approximately 225 Earth days. It follows a nearly circular orbit compared with many other planetary orbits.

Slow Rotation

Venus takes approximately 243 Earth days to rotate once on its axis. This rotation is retrograde, meaning Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.

Day and Night

Because Venus rotates so slowly, one rotation takes longer than one trip around the Sun. Its solar day, measured from one sunrise to the next, is about 117 Earth days.

No Natural Moons

Venus has no known natural moons. It is one of the two planets in our solar system without a natural satellite; the other is Mercury.

06 / WHY VENUS MATTERS

Importance to Earth

Studying Venus helps scientists understand planetary climates, atmospheric evolution, and the conditions that make worlds habitable.

🌍

Earth's Sister Planet

Venus is similar to Earth in size and rocky composition, making the differences between the two planets especially interesting.

🌡️

Climate Research

Venus provides an important example of an extreme greenhouse effect and helps scientists investigate how planetary atmospheres evolve.

🔭

Planetary Science

Studying Venus helps researchers compare rocky planets and investigate the roles of atmosphere, geology, and solar energy.

07 / EXPLORING VENUS

Space Missions

Spacecraft have helped reveal Venus's atmosphere, surface, and geological history.

Mariner 2

Launched by NASA in 1962, Mariner 2 became the first spacecraft to successfully fly past another planet. It provided important measurements of Venus.

Venera Missions

The Soviet Venera program sent spacecraft to Venus. Some landers returned data from the surface despite the extreme temperature and pressure.

Magellan

NASA's Magellan spacecraft mapped much of Venus's surface using radar, revealing extensive volcanic and geological features.

Akatsuki

Japan's Akatsuki mission studied Venus's atmosphere and weather patterns, helping scientists investigate its unusual cloud movements and atmospheric circulation.

Future exploration: Planned missions such as NASA's DAVINCI and VERITAS and ESA's EnVision aim to investigate Venus's atmosphere, surface, and geological evolution. Mission schedules can change over time.
08 / DID YOU KNOW?

Interesting Facts About Venus

Discover some surprising facts about this bright and mysterious planet.

✨

Bright in Our Sky

Venus is often the brightest planet visible from Earth. It is commonly seen near sunrise or sunset and is sometimes called the Morning Star or Evening Star.

🔄

Backward Rotation

Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets. If you could stand on its surface and see through its clouds, the Sun would appear to rise in the west.

⏳

A Long Rotation

Venus takes longer to rotate once on its axis than it takes to complete one orbit around the Sun.

🌡️

Hottest Planet

Despite not being the closest planet to the Sun, Venus has the hottest planetary surface in our solar system because of its intense greenhouse effect.

🌑

No Moons

Venus has no known natural satellites. Its skies and surface are very different from Earth's familiar environment.

🛰️

Radar Reveals Its Surface

Thick clouds block ordinary visible-light views of the surface, so radar has played a major role in mapping Venus.
